### [1] [Cyclotron: A Recurrence Language for Expressing Interprocessor Communication](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20981)
*Shiv Sundram,Akhilesh Balasingam,Nathan Zhang,Kunle Olukotun,Fredrik Kjolstad*

Main category: cs.DC

TL;DR: Cyclotron 是一个以递推方程（recurrence equations）描述流式数据流算法的编译器，将逻辑迭代空间上的递推语言降为相互连接处理器组成的分布式拓扑，每个处理器程序包含 send/receive/计算。其关键点是通信在递推语言中显式表达、与硬件映射解耦，用户只需选择哪些维度映射到处理器、哪些映射到时间（space-time mapping），编译器自动完成通信与计算算子的正确放置。


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: 分布式线性代数库（ScaLAPACK / SLATE / cuSolverMp）把算法与通信模式固化在一起，用户难以表达非标准通信模式（最近邻脉动交换、one-to-all 广播、双向 split ring、非对称分段广播），也难以在不重写程序的情况下改变空间/时间映射。论文希望把"算法+通信"与"映射到硬件"这两件事解耦。

Method: 1) 语言：以递推方程描述算法，数据不仅可来自外部内存访问，还可"驻留"在迭代空间上，内部点之间可直接通信；用户在递推中直接指定通信。2) 编译：将逻辑迭代空间上的递推降为逐处理器程序（send/recv/compute）；用户仅指定跨迭代空间的数据依赖，并选择处理器维度 vs 时间维度，编译器依据 space-time 映射安全生成每进程程序并正确放置通信与计算算子。3) 评测：分布式 GEMM、TRSM、Cholesky、融合 Cholesky-TRSM，最多 16 CPU 节点与 16 GPU，对比 ScaLAPACK、SLATE、cuSolverMp。

Result: Cyclotron 与手写优化库相比具备竞争力，且常能超越。对 Cholesky，最优通信策略随问题规模与体系结构变化（例如小规模/GPU 上最优策略与小规模 CPU 集群不同），说明"一次编写、按映射重配置"的价值。

Conclusion: 主要贡献是将通信模式提升为一等公民、与硬件映射正交，并通过 space-time 映射由编译器自动生成分布式程序，从而在不重写代码的前提下探索不同通信策略与部署形态。工程意义：为分布式算子/通信自动生成提供了一条"算法即递推 + 映射可配置"的路径，可用于快速原型化非标准通信模式、针对规模与架构自动选策略，以及融合算子（如 Cholesky-TRSM）的端到端生成。

Abstract: We present Cyclotron, a compiler that expresses streaming dataflow algorithms using recurrence equations, which are then compiled to distributed topologies of interlinked processors. Our compiler lowers a language of recurrences over logical iteration spaces into per-processor programs of sends, receives, and computations. In these recurrences, data accesses are not limited to external accesses to memory; data can live separately on the iteration space itself, allowing internal points to communicate values directly with other points. Users specify this communication directly in the recurrence language, which allows them to express various communication patterns such as a nearest-neighbor systolic exchange, a one-to-all broadcast, a bidirectional split ring, or an asymmetric and piecewise broadcast pattern, which are often not supported by standard distributed linear algebra libraries. These recurrences describe the algorithm and communication independently of how they map onto the hardware. A programmer then chooses which dimensions execute across processors and which execute over time, without rewriting the entire program. Users only specify data dependencies across the iteration space, and the compiler safely generates a program, per process, with correct placement of both communication and computational operators according to the chosen space--time mapping. We evaluate distributed matrix multiplication, TRSM, Cholesky, and fused Cholesky--TRSM on up to 16 CPU nodes and 16 GPUs, comparing against ScaLAPACK, SLATE, and cuSolverMp. Cyclotron is competitive with and often outperforms these hand-optimized libraries; for Cholesky, the best communication strategy changes with problem size and architecture.

We also identify a memory-feasibility boundary: a p99 cap for high-variance or OOM-prone data bounds outliers before BCL balances the rest.
